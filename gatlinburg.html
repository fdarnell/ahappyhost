What a concierge does that a management company doesn't

A management company runs the rental for you: listings, pricing, bookings, guest messages, cleaners. For that, published rates from Smokies management companies run from about 20% to 40% of your rental revenue, and every booking pays it whether anything needed doing or not.

A concierge works the other way around. You keep running the business, or you keep the manager you already have, and we handle the jobs that need someone standing in the cabin. Owning a cabin in Gatlinburg comes with its own paperwork

The first question is which side of the city line your cabin sits on. A Gatlinburg mailing address covers a lot of mountain that isn't inside the city, so check the Sevier County map before you assume anything.

Inside city limits, the city's own checklist for overnight rentals asks for a city business license, a county business license, a state sales tax number and a Tourist Residency Permit. The permit fee is $200 for up to two bedrooms plus $75 for each extra bedroom, and it pays for a fire and building inspection of the cabin. Here's the line owners miss: Airbnb and VRBO collect the sales tax and Gatlinburg's 3% hotel tax for you, but not the city's 1.25% gross receipts tax. You file that one yourself by the 20th of every month.

Outside city limits, the Sevier County Fire Marshal's Short-Term Rental Unit permit applies: $250 a year for up to 12 guests, an inspection before it's issued and another every year after. The county checks smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, fire extinguishers on every level and escape routes.

Gatlinburg cabins, from Chalet Village to the Glades

Gatlinburg rentals sit in very different places. There are the hillside cabins in Chalet Village and up Ski Mountain Road toward Ober, the homes off Historic Nature Trail near the Roaring Fork entrance, the cabins along the Glades and the Arts & Crafts loop, and resort communities like Cobbly Nob out US 321. A setup at the top of a steep switchback driveway is a different job from one at a condo off the Parkway.

Getting there is the other half. Our office is in Pigeon Forge, about eight miles from downtown Gatlinburg and roughly 17 minutes with no traffic. The Gatlinburg Spur carries around 40,000 vehicles a day in summer, and October is the park's busiest month. When snow hits, as it did in January and February of 2025, the city warns of hazardous roads and stops the trolleys. That's exactly when an owner who lives six hours away needs someone already here.

Questions Gatlinburg owners ask us

Is A Happy Host a property management company?

No. We don't list your cabin, take bookings or message your guests, and we don't take a percentage of your rental income. We're the local team you call for the hands-on work: setting a cabin up, inspecting it, fixing and decorating it, and showing up when something goes wrong.

Can I keep my management company and still use you?

Yes, and some owners do exactly that. One owner reached out because their management company didn't have enough weekend coverage. Others use their manager for bookings and call us for holiday decor or a new-cabin setup.

What does the City of Gatlinburg require from short-term rental owners?

Inside city limits, the city's own checklist lists a city business license, a Sevier County business license, a state sales tax number and a Tourist Residency Permit, which pays for a fire and building inspection of the cabin. The zoning has to allow overnight rentals before you apply. Owners also file Gatlinburg's 1.25% gross receipts tax every month; Airbnb and VRBO don't file that one for you. Confirm current requirements with the city's Finance Department at 865-436-1405.

My cabin has a Gatlinburg address but sits outside city limits. Which rules apply?

Sevier County's. Plenty of cabins with a Gatlinburg mailing address are in the county, and since January 2024 those need a Short-Term Rental Unit permit from the Sevier County Fire Marshal: $250 a year for up to 12 guests, with an inspection before the permit is issued and again every year. The county's map will tell you which side of the line you're on.

How quickly can you get to a cabin in Gatlinburg?

Our office is about eight miles from downtown Gatlinburg, roughly a 17-minute drive with no traffic. On a summer Saturday on the Spur, or up a steep road after snow, it takes longer, and we'll tell you honestly when to expect us.
